## SUMMARY
Google Play ranking in 2025-2026 is a two-stage system: text relevance from the indexed fields (Title 30 chars > Short Description 80 > Full Description 4,000, plus category, up-to-five tags, promo cards, and data-safety content — developer name is not meaningfully indexed) gates entry to the result pool, then post-install behavior ranks within it. Google has made the behavioral side unusually explicit with published numeric bars: user-perceived crash rate 1.09% overall / 8% per-device, ANR 0.47% / 8%, plus new 'Core Value' thresholds (DAU/MAU below 8% or user-loss/uninstall rate above 5% triggers listing warnings and surface ineligibility) — official confirmation that vitals, engagement, and uninstalls directly gate discoverability. The 2024-2026 rebalance moved weight from install volume to retention (healthy band: D1 >35%, D7 >15%), and keyword best practice tightened to 2-3 natural mentions with active stuffing penalties (one vendor still recommends repetition, noted as a conflict). The bigger structural shift is AI as the discovery intermediary: AI review summaries went broad Oct-Nov 2025, and Google I/O 2026 shipped Ask Play conversational search, Gemini discovery beyond the store, Play Shorts, and Gemini-generated localized listings — making reviews function as positioning data and the long description source material for AI-written highlights. Promotional content (LiveOps) is the one Google-quantified Explore lever: 2x explore acquisitions during featuring windows, median +106% uplift. For Sniffy specifically, nearly everything needed is free: the public batchexecute endpoints (via google-play-scraper) expose per-locale listings, ratings histograms, paginated per-language reviews, autocomplete, similar apps, and data safety — enough to ship Android review intelligence, localization gap analysis, a vitals-proxy quality score, and an AI-comprehension grade, none of which competitors offer in agent-accessible form; only true vitals, retention, conversion, and the search/explore acquisition split remain Console-only.

## FINDINGS
- [high] Google Play indexes the Title (30 chars), Short Description (80), Full Description (4,000), category, up-to-five developer tags, promotional content cards, and even data-safety-section content — and one 2026 industry analysis found 84.2% of successful ranking improvements correlated with adding the target keyword to the Short Description even when the Title was unchanged.
  AppRadar's 2026 ranking-factors guide (combining SplitMetrics/App Radar data across thousands of apps) lists 16 factors and links Google's official listing doc (support.google.com answer/9958766) as the source for indexed fields. Title carries the most weight; the Short Description is called 'where the next ranking gain hides' because a keyword that doesn't fit the 30-char Title often fits the 80-char field and 'Google's algorithm rewards that placement strongly.' Long Description follows, with the first lines weighted more than the rest. Tags and category 'assign your app to the right neighborhoods' but do not directly carry keyword weight. Developer name was notably absent from every indexed-field list in 2025-2026 sources (unlike older ASO lore).
  src: https://appradar.com/academy/app-store-ranking-factors
- [medium] Current keyword-density best practice on Play is 2-3 natural mentions of the primary keyword across metadata; Google now actively penalizes stuffing, retiring the older 'repeat 3-5 times' advice — though AppTweak (June 2025) still recommends 'strategically repeating your main keywords a few times' in the long description.
  AppRadar (2026): 'Current best practice is 2 to 3 natural mentions of your primary keyword, not the older repeat 3 to 5 times advice. Google now penalizes keyword stuffing.' AppTweak's June 23, 2025 ranking-factors article partially conflicts: it says Google indexes the long description and 'strategically repeating your main keywords a few times within the text will strengthen your relevance signal,' while warning against stuffing. The two agree on direction (moderate repetition helps, stuffing hurts) but differ on how aggressive the 2026 penalty is — the 2026 source claims comprehension-style scoring is replacing raw frequency. Both are vendor blogs, not Google statements.
  src: https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/google-play-ranking-factors
- [high] Google officially publishes hard Android vitals 'bad behavior thresholds' that gate Play visibility: user-perceived crash rate 1.09% overall / 8% per-device, user-perceived ANR rate 0.47% overall / 8% per-device; exceeding them makes the app 'less discoverable,' can trigger a public warning on the store listing, and causes Play to steer users on affected device models toward competitors.
  developer.android.com/topic/performance/vitals shows the threshold table (crash 1.09% overall / 8% per-device / 4% per-watch; ANR 0.47% / 8% / 5%) and states core vitals 'affect your app's visibility on Google Play.' The Play Console help page defines the overall threshold as being in the bottom 25% of the top 1,000 apps by installs, and confirms per-device-model steering plus listing warnings. Excessive partial wake locks is a third core vital but is in beta and does not yet reduce discoverability. Note: AppTweak's 2025 article misstates this as '8% for crash and ANR rates' — that is only the per-device threshold, not the much stricter overall one.
  src: https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/vitals
- [high] Google now also publishes 'Core Value' thresholds tied directly to engagement and uninstalls: if DAU/MAU falls below 8%, or 'user loss rate' (users who uninstalled from all devices vs. users with the app on an active device) exceeds 5%, a warning may be shown on the store listing and the app may become ineligible for certain Play surfaces.
  From the official Android vitals help page (answer/9844486): 'When your app's DAU / MAU does not meet the 8% threshold for core value, a warning may be shown on your store listing, and your app may not be eligible to appear on certain surfaces on Google Play,' and identically for user loss rate above 5%. This is Google's first explicit, numeric confirmation that uninstall rate and engagement gate browse/recommendation surfaces — previously this was only inferred by ASO vendors. MAU is defined over a 28-day window; a device counts as active if turned on in the past 30 days.
  src: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9844486
- [medium] Between 2024 and 2026 Google rebalanced Play ranking away from pre-install signals (keywords, raw installs) toward post-install signals — retention, engagement, vitals — with healthy bands cited as Day-1 retention above 35% and Day-7 above 15%.
  AppRadar quotes Eric Seufert (Mobile Dev Memo) describing the shift as moving 'from ranking by install volume to ranking by retention and engagement,' and reports apps improving on Android vitals see measurable ranking gains with unchanged metadata, while apps with rough vitals lose visibility regardless of listing quality. MobileAction independently lists uninstall rate, DAU, session length/frequency as demotion/promotion signals ('if many users install an app but quickly abandon or remove it, the algorithm may demote it'). These are vendor syntheses, but they align with Google's published Core Value thresholds above.
  src: https://appradar.com/academy/app-store-ranking-factors
- [high] AI-generated review summaries ('Users are saying') rolled out broadly on Google Play in Oct-Nov 2025 (Play Store v48.5.23-31): a single balanced paragraph distilling praise and criticism, shown only for apps with 'substantial review data.'
  The rollout was confirmed Oct 31, 2025 (updated Nov 12, 2025). Summaries appear in the ratings & reviews section; Google uses a simpler single-paragraph approach vs. Apple's multi-stage topic-modeling pipeline. The article also cites University of Hawaii research showing AI summaries reduce the number and length of subsequent reviews and correlate with higher average ratings — creating a feedback loop where the summary shapes the future review pool. Known defects: near-identical summaries across different apps and occasional factual errors.
  src: https://android.gadgethacks.com/news/google-play-store-ai-review-summaries-roll-out-now/
- [high] Google I/O 2026 (May 2026) made AI the discovery layer: Ask Play conversational search inside the store, Gemini-powered app discovery on Android and the web, Play Shorts (TikTok-style vertical video feed, US select developers), and Gemini tools in Play Console that generate localized listings and store-listing variants tied to keyword recommendations.
  Per Appbot's May 22, 2026 analysis of Google's I/O announcements (android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/05/io-2026-whats-new-in-google-play.html): Ask Play supports multi-turn refinement; Google says its existing AI Q&A ('Ask Play about this app', Gemini-powered) already answers the majority of user questions on listings. Critically, early examples suggest Ask Play draws on public information sources beyond the Play listing itself — developer websites and customer-facing content — so listing/website positioning consistency becomes a ranking-adjacent concern. This complements the 2025 'Guided Search' change that groups results by user intent/goals (e.g. 'learn Spanish') rather than flat keyword lists, and the personalized 'You' tab.
  src: https://appbot.co/blog/google-ask-play/
- [medium] In the conversational-discovery era, reviews function as large-scale user-generated positioning data: the vocabulary users repeat in reviews ('easy to use', 'good for beginners') feeds how Gemini/Ask Play categorize and recommend an app, and developer review replies become public trust signals.
  Appbot's analysis: 'reviews increasingly function like large-scale user-generated positioning data... Repeated complaints do not just lower ratings. Over time, they may also influence how AI systems interpret where an app is or is not a good fit.' Semantic reinforcement matters — if screenshots say 'simple budgeting for students' and reviews repeatedly say 'easy to use,' the signals compound. Google also surfaces AI-generated 'app highlights' extracted from developer descriptions (androidpolice.com/google-play-store-apps-highlights-ai/), meaning the long description is now source material for machine-written listing UI, not just an index target.
  src: https://appbot.co/blog/google-ask-play/
- [high] Google's own data: apps that submit promotional content (LiveOps) and request featuring see over 2x explore acquisitions during a featuring window, with a median uplift of 106% among statistically significant events; closed-beta participants saw +5% active users and +4% revenue.
  From the official Play Console promotional-content page: the 2x figure comes from ~6k high-quality events submitted by 2k+ titles, Nov 2023-Jan 2024 (just over half had statistically significant uplift; median 106%). 'Running high-quality and frequent promotional content is an important signal to Google Play that you're continually investing in your app.' Promo cards appear on the store listing, homepage, and search results. Access is gated behind 'premium growth tools' eligibility criteria, and Gemini can now auto-generate the promo assets. Content rules require unique assets per event and 24h+ lead time for review.
  src: https://play.google.com/console/about/programs/promotionalcontent/
- [high] Play's metadata policy (enforced since Sept 29, 2021, fully rolled out Dec 15, 2021) caps titles at 30 chars and bans performance/price/promo words — 'free', 'best', 'top', 'new', '#1', 'download now' — across title, icon, developer name, and effectively all metadata; enforcement ranges from Console warnings to loss-of-promotion eligibility to outright suspension of smaller apps.
  AppRadar's enforcement study documented Console warnings citing 'possible loss of promotion' for non-compliant short descriptions, release rejections for the word 'new' in long descriptions, and developer reports of small apps suspended over 'free'/'best' in titles. It also found a buffer effect: when myposter removed 'Fotos drucken' from its title but increased the keyword's repetitions in the long description, rankings recovered to above-prior levels — evidence that long-description repetition partially substitutes for title placement. The penalty for title violations is harsher than for description violations.
  src: https://appradar.com/blog/google-play-policy-update-2021-impact-aso
- [medium] Google auto-localizes short descriptions (near-100% apparent coverage: 88% apps / 94% games in the US), but only 59% of apps vs 69% of games manually localize their titles — and localized listings get a measurable ranking advantage per market, with ratings and keyword rankings being country-specific.
  AppTweak's ASO Report 2025 data; AppTweak explicitly recommends overriding Google's machine-localized short descriptions for cultural relevancy — meaning a scraper can detect 'machine-translated short description + English title' as a localization gap. MobileAction confirms Google 'recognizes when an app listing is tailored to a region, and often gives a ranking advantage to localized apps.' Play ratings have displayed per-country/per-device-form-factor since late 2021/early 2022 (rollout date from background knowledge, not the cited source — the cited sources confirm the per-country state, not the date).
  src: https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/google-play-ranking-factors
- [low] Search remains the biggest Play discovery channel (~65% of downloads per 2026 industry figures), but Explore (browse: Top Charts, Featured, Similar Apps, personalized collections) is a major and growing source, especially for games — and Similar Apps placement is driven by category, comparable download volume, ratings, vitals, and visual/text similarity.
  AppRadar 2026 cites ~65% of downloads via search store-wide. MobileAction confirms the three-way split: Search, Explore, and external referrals, each weighted differently by the algorithm. The Similar Apps mechanics (Google image-recognition on icons/screenshots, language recognition on metadata and review text, download-volume parity) come from an Incipia/TheTool analysis that is pre-2024 (2020) — directionally useful but stale; treat the specific Similar Apps factor list as low confidence. Google's official promo-content page separately confirms 'explore acquisitions' is a tracked acquisition class that featuring directly multiplies.
  src: https://incipia.co/post/guest-posts/everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-explore-traffic-on-google-play-store/
- [high] Nearly the entire public Play listing surface is scrapeable without authentication via the batchexecute endpoints wrapped by facundoolano/google-play-scraper (8.9k dependents): per-lang/per-country app details, ratings histogram, paginated reviews (150/page with continuation tokens, per-language, sortable by newest/rating/helpfulness), search, autocomplete suggest, similar apps, permissions, and the data safety section.
  The `app` method takes `lang` + `country` (hl/gl) and returns title, summary (short description), full description, histogram {1-5 star counts}, installs, score, recentChanges, etc. — enabling per-locale listing diffs. `reviews` paginates 150/page with nextPaginationToken and is per-language ('you need to try different languages to get more reviews'); review counts shown on the listing are ratings counts, not written-review counts. `suggest` returns up to 5 autocomplete completions per term/lang/country. Console-only (NOT scrapeable): Android vitals, retention curves, uninstall/user-loss rate, DAU/MAU, search-vs-explore acquisition split, conversion rates, and store listing experiments.
  src: https://github.com/facundoolano/google-play-scraper
- [high] Google's official listing-content guidance (the doc ASO vendors cite for indexed fields) is support.google.com answer/9958766 and answer/13393723 — Title 30 / Short 80 / Full 4,000, with Google itself warning against keyword stuffing and 'repetitive blocks' and emphasizing clarity over frequency.
  Jen Li's 2025 metadata guide and AppRadar both anchor on these Google docs: 'the indexed text signals are your Title, Short Description, and Full Description (with strong emphasis on Title/Short).' The same guide notes Play offers up to 50 Custom Store Listings (by country or audience, routable via unique URLs) — a localization/targeting surface that is partially visible from outside (the default per-locale listing) but whose custom-audience variants are not scrapeable. This contrasts with iOS, where the description is not indexed at all; on Play the 4,000-char description is both indexed and now the source for AI-generated app highlights.
  src: https://jenli.net/tpost/atx9hpv6n1-app-store-and-google-play-metadata-writi

- (free-data) Android review intelligence: fetch Play reviews via the public paginated endpoint (150/page, per-language) and extract 'positioning vocabulary' (recurring user phrases), crash/ANR complaint rate, and sentiment themes — then diff that vocabulary against the listing's title/short/long description.
  WHY: Sniffy currently has zero Play review fetching, and post-I/O-2026 reviews are literal discovery data (Gemini/Ask Play interpret review language to decide when to recommend an app). Appbot charges seat-license dashboards for exactly this analysis with no API/MCP; AppTweak's review tools are enterprise-tier. An agent-buyable 'does your review language match your listing language' signal does not exist anywhere.
  DATA: google-play-scraper `reviews` (free, unauthenticated, already a known-good library — repo README cached at /Users/theointech/Projects/ASOSniffy/asosniffy-com/.firecrawl/gplay-readme.md); LLM pass for theme extraction.
- (free-data) Android localization gap analysis: call the `app` endpoint across hl/gl combinations, detect machine-translated short descriptions paired with un-localized English titles (Google auto-translates short descriptions, so 'translated summary + English title' is the fingerprint of a neglected locale), and score locale coverage against the markets in the user's keyword set.
  WHY: Directly extends Sniffy's existing iOS multi-storefront localization analysis (franc) to Android, closing a stated product gap. Only 59% of apps localize titles, and localized listings get a documented ranking advantage — this is a high-yield, deterministic recommendation competitors bury inside $400+/mo dashboard tiers (MobileAction 'Localization' tool).
  DATA: google-play-scraper `app` with lang/country params (free); existing franc language-detection pipeline reused from the iOS path.
- (free-data) Quality-bar proxy score: Sniffy can't see Android vitals, but it can (a) detect the public 'bad behavior' warning badge on the scraped listing, (b) compute a crash/ANR complaint rate from recent reviews, and (c) report Google's exact published thresholds (1.09% crash / 0.47% ANR / 8% DAU-MAU / 5% user-loss) as the quality bars the developer must check in Play Console — with provenance label `inferred`.
  WHY: No ASO tool exposes a vitals-proxy from public data; they all either require Play Console OAuth (enterprise integrations) or ignore vitals entirely. Since vitals + Core Value now gate discoverability with published numeric thresholds, telling an indie 'your reviews show 4.1% of recent reviewers mention crashes — Google's visibility threshold is 1.09% user-perceived' is uniquely actionable and honest about provenance.
  DATA: Play listing scrape (warning badge) + review keyword classification (free); Google's threshold table is static public documentation.
- (vision-llm) AI-comprehension listing score: run an LLM extraction over the scraped listing asking 'what does this app do, for whom, and what are its 3 highlights' — exactly what Gemini's app highlights and Ask Play do — and grade how well the extraction matches the developer's intended positioning and target keywords.
  WHY: Post-I/O 2026, listings are read by Gemini before they're read by humans (Ask Play, AI highlights, AI review summaries). Every competitor still scores listings on keyword density; nobody scores 'will the AI intermediary describe your app correctly.' This is a natural paid-report section for Sniffy and is inherently agent-native framing.
  DATA: Already-scraped listing text + one LLM call per report (cheap, fits existing AI-synthesis step). No new data source.
- (free-data) Short-description keyword coverage + policy linter: deterministic checks flagging (1) target keywords absent from the 80-char short description (84.2% of successful ranking improvements correlated with adding them there), (2) stuffing (>3 mentions of one keyword across metadata), and (3) banned policy terms ('free', 'best', 'top', '#1', 'download now') that risk warnings, lost promotion eligibility, or suspension.
  WHY: Pure-text checks with documented enforcement consequences that big tools surface only inside paid audit dashboards; trivially explainable in a report and perfect for the free-quote shallowScan teaser on Android (identity + one flagged policy violation creates instant value-before-wallet).
  DATA: Already-scraped listing metadata (free); static banned-word list from Google's metadata policy; no external API.
- (hard) Competitor investment signal: detect whether competing apps run promotional-content cards/events on their listings and surface 'competitor X is running LiveOps (Google: 2x explore acquisitions during featuring, median +106%) — you are not' in the competitor trail.
  WHY: Promo content is one of the few official, Google-quantified visibility levers, and AppTweak tracks it only in its top-tier product. Even a binary present/absent flag per competitor is a differentiated signal no API-first tool sells; it also reinforces Sniffy's 'best next sniff' recommendation framing.
  DATA: Play listing scrape — promo/event cards render in listing HTML but are JS-heavy and algorithmically targeted, so detection may be partial (label `inferred` when absent); free but engineering-medium.

  DATA: google-play-scraper `suggest` with term/lang/country (free); existing Phase 9 relevance-gate pipeline.
