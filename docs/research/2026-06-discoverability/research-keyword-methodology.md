## SUMMARY
iOS "search popularity" everywhere in ASO tooling is Apple's Search Ads popularity index — officially documented by Apple only as a 1-5 dot bidding aid; the 5-100 integer tools display is an undocumented internal value with no public methodology, reachable only through campaign-scoped ASA surfaces or internal dashboard endpoints (this repo's own 2026-06-03 probing confirmed the public Campaign Management API v5 has no campaign-free popularity endpoint: /keywords/recommendations 404s). The metric publicly collapsed in October 2025 (US keywords above floor fell 77.4% in four days; Apple reportedly stopped exposing scores below 50), splitting vendors into exposed ASA-passthroughs (AppTweak, AppFollow, App Radar, Appfigures, MobileAction), hybrids (ASOdesk, Phiture), and independent models (APPlyzer, Gummicube DATACUBE, Sensor Tower's 10M-consumer panel). Accuracy ground truth: SplitMetrics + Phiture empirically fit max daily impressions = 254.44 × e^(0.0615 × SP) with a ~4-day lag (2019 US data — the only published mapping). For Google Play there is no official number at all; AppTweak's stated method is autocomplete suggestions cross-referenced against Apple's popularity for the same terms, while others use panels or unstated models with no published error bars. Difficulty scores are converging on one shape: aggregate strength (downloads/ratings/age/title-match) of the top-10 ranked apps, optionally made app-relative (AppTweak's Chance, KEI = volume × chance) — with a known brand-term blind spot. The best documented free-proxy methodology is open source (RespectASO: six iTunes-Search-API signals for popularity, seven for difficulty), and no one has published a validation of autocomplete suggestion order against ASA popularity — an open gap Sniffy could fill cheaply and own. Competitor access is seat-priced and agent-hostile (SensorTower API enterprise-only at $2,000+/mo; AppTweak API a $166+/mo add-on); the one per-request alternative (an Apify actor at $20/1,000 keywords scraping Apple's internal API) is now deprecated, leaving the agent-buyable per-keyword niche empty. Net for Sniffy: double down on documented observable-signal scoring with provenance labels (the thing that broke in Oct 2025 was exactly the dependency Sniffy doesn't have), add difficulty/chance and impressions-translation from free data, and price a standalone x402 keyword endpoint into the vacuum the Apify actor left.

## FINDINGS
- [high] Apple's only public definition of Search Popularity is a 1-to-5 dot scale inside paid Apple Ads documentation; the 5-100 integer that ASO tools display is an undocumented underlying value with no published methodology, changelog, or stability guarantee.
  The Apple Ads help glossary states 'Search popularity is displayed as numbers from 1 to 5, with 5 being the most popular.' Every Apple reference to the metric sits in paid Apple Ads docs (Reporting, Recommendations, Glossary) next to bidding inputs like recommended max CPT. Apple's organic-search developer page lists ranking factors (text relevance in title/subtitle/keywords/category plus downloads, ratings, reviews) and never mentions ASA popularity. The 5-100 scale tools show is the raw integer behind the dots, retrofitted by the industry into an organic keyword-research foundation it was never designed for.
  src: https://respectaso.com/blog/apple-search-ads-popularity-unreliable-aso-keyword-data/
- [high] First-party empirical probing (this repo, 2026-06-03) confirms the public Apple Ads Campaign Management API v5 exposes no campaign-free keyword-popularity endpoint: POST /api/v5/keywords/recommendations returns 404 RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND, alternates 404/410/503, while GET /api/v5/search/apps works without a campaign.
  With valid OAuth (client-assertion JWT, GET /api/v5/acls 200), /api/v5/keywords/recommendations 404s because Apple parses 'recommendations' as a keyword ID; /api/v5/search/keywords 404s, /api/v4/* is 410 Gone, /api/v5/keyword-recommendations and /recommendations/keywords 503 with no gateway route. The popularity bars in the ASA UI come from internal dashboard endpoints, not the public API. Only /api/v5/search/apps (ad-catalog app lookup returning adamId/appName/developerName/countryOrRegionCodes) is available campaign-free, now used for catalogVerified app-identity stamping. This partially conflicts with RespectASO's phrasing that the 5-100 integer is 'returned by the Apple Ads Campaign Management API' — reconciliation: the value flows only through campaign-scoped Recommendations/Reporting contexts or internal dashboard endpoints, and tools that scrape the dashboard 'rely on session cookies that break under 2FA' (RespectASO).

- [high] In late September-October 2025 the ASA popularity metric publicly broke: US keywords with popularity above 5 fell from 165,875 to 39,254 in four days (-77.4%), with most keywords previously scoring 20-60 pinned at the floor value of 5, affecting every ASA-derived ASO tool.
  ASO.dev measured the collapse and verified values came directly from Apple's API. MobileAction ('affects all ASO tools in the market that derive their data from Apple Ads'), APPlyzer, Astro's founder, and Appfigures all corroborated. Phiture's ASO Monthly 113 reported industry consensus that Apple stopped displaying search scores below 50. Appfigures said it 'identified another way to collect popularity scores' (source unstated). Apple issued no acknowledgement. A precursor happened June 2024 when Apple began returning app-relevant SAP instead of global SAP, flooring 'irrelevant' keywords at 5.
  src: https://respectaso.com/blog/apple-search-ads-popularity-unreliable-aso-keyword-data/
- [high] Commercial vendors split into three camps for iOS popularity: direct ASA passthrough (AppTweak, App Radar, AppFollow, Appfigures, MobileAction), ASA-as-one-input hybrids (ASOdesk Traffic Score, Phiture), and independent models (APPlyzer proprietary model, Gummicube DATACUBE, Sensor Tower's 10M+ consumer panel).
  Quoted from vendors' own pages: AppTweak's iOS volume is 'directly fetched from Apple's Search Popularity metric'; AppFollow: 'The Apple Search Ads score and our Popularity Score are the same values'; App Radar: 'we pull keyword popularity values directly from Apple.' APPlyzer during the collapse: 'uses its own independent and dynamic model... doesn't rely on Apple's API values.' Sensor Tower's Traffic Score is proprietary, informed by a panel of more than 10 million consumers. Passthrough vendors were the most exposed by the 2025 collapse; MobileAction publicly froze popularity updates.
  src: https://respectaso.com/blog/apple-search-ads-popularity-unreliable-aso-keyword-data/
- [high] The only published empirical accuracy mapping for ASA popularity is SplitMetrics + Phiture's study: max daily App Store impressions = 254.4443 * exp(0.0615 * SearchPopularity), with popularity lagging impressions by about 4 days (likely a 7-day moving average).
  Based on 30,805 US search terms, daily data Aug-Oct 2019, 315,993 observations (published/maintained on splitmetrics.com, article dated 2023). Lookup table: SP 10 = ~500 impressions/day, 20 = 900, 30 = 1,600, 40 = 3,000, 50 = 5,500, 60 = 10,000, 70 = 19,000, 80 = 35,000; SP 5-20 is 'close to zero' in raw data; outliers like 'instagram'/'snapchat' (SP 98-99) reach ~0.5M/day. Flag: underlying data is 2019 (pre-2024) and pre-dates both the June 2024 SAP-logic shift and the Oct 2025 collapse, so the curve's floor behavior is now stale even though the exponential shape remains the canonical reference.
  src: https://splitmetrics.com/blog/apple-search-popularity-index/
- [high] Appfigures documents the popularity score as a relative 5-100 index (0 = keyword not understood by the algorithm at all) and found in a ~11,000-keyword US App Store study that most keywords score between 10 and 40.
  Their guide states 100 = very popular (e.g. 'Instagram'), 5 = little-to-no searches, and 'many keywords you'll look at will have a popularity score of 5.' Their advice threshold: single-digit popularity keywords are not worth targeting. Their Competitiveness Score summarizes estimated downloads, category rank, ratings, and age of the top-10 ranked apps, on the premise that 'Apple's and Google's algorithms use downloads to determine the order in which to display results.'
  src: https://appfigures.com/resources/guides/which-keywords-to-optimize-for/amp
- [high] AppTweak's keyword Difficulty is the aggregate 'App Power' of the top-10 apps ranking on the keyword; 'Chance' compares your app's App Power against those top-10; KEI (Keyword Efficiency Index) combines volume with chance — and branded terms produce misleading difficulty (e.g. 'waze' KD 47).
  From AppTweak's own product methodology post (last updated July 8, 2025): if top-10 apps all have high App Power (their download/performance-based 0-100 KPI), difficulty is high. Chance is explicitly app-relative, not keyword-absolute. KEI = best combination of high volume and high chance. They warn that branded keywords look artificially easy because the difficulty formula doesn't model brand intent — competitors ranking on 'waze' at KD 47 still can't win it, and adding brand terms to metadata violates store policy.
  src: https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/ranking-difficulty-chance-score-now-on-apptweak
- [high] For Google Play, where no official search-volume number exists, AppTweak states it uses 'a proprietary system based on auto-suggestions and cross-referencing Apple's Search Popularity data,' and frames competing tools as relying on 'less sophisticated modeling that produces inconsistent results.'
  From AppTweak's 2026 keyword-tools guide: 'For iOS, the most accurate tools pull directly from Apple's Search Popularity metric... For Google Play, where official keyword data doesn't exist, ask how the platform estimates search volume.' This confirms cross-store transfer (Apple SP as ground truth for Play estimates of the same terms) plus autocomplete signals as the dominant commercial Play methodology. Sensor Tower instead leans on its consumer panel, and Gummicube markets DATACUBE as 'real mobile data, not web data' — an implicit dig at tools that recycle Google Keyword Planner web volumes. No vendor publishes Play error bars; accuracy is unverifiable by design.
  src: https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/best-aso-keyword-research-tools
- [high] A gray-market Apify actor ('App Store Keyword Popularity Scores & ASO API') sold popularity scores 'direct from Apple Search Ads internal API' at $20 per 1,000 keyword analyses across 57 storefronts with no Apple account — and is now marked Deprecated.
  The actor returned popularity (5-100), a computed 0-100 difficulty 'calibrated against professional ASO tools (r=0.87, MAE=6.5)', top ranking apps, related searches (Apple autocomplete), and 30-80 keyword recommendations per seed term with popularity scores. Its existence proves the internal-dashboard-API route works technically and that a per-request, agent-friendly price point (~$0.02/keyword) is viable; its deprecation (cause unstated, plausibly the Oct 2025 changes or Apple enforcement) proves the route is fragile. The page also tabulates competitor API pricing: SensorTower $2,000+/mo enterprise-only, AppTweak $79-549/mo with API an extra $166+/mo, MobileAction $69-239/mo enterprise-only API.
  src: https://apify.com/asomaniac/app-store-keyword-tool
- [high] RespectASO ships a fully open-source free-proxy methodology: popularity estimated from six observable iTunes Search API signals (result count, leader strength by rating volume, title-match density, market depth, specificity penalty, exact-phrase bonus), with difficulty from seven separate factors.
  Difficulty factors: rating volume, dominant players, rating quality, market maturity, publisher diversity, app count, content relevance. The README states 'RespectASO uses the iTunes Search API as its only data source — no Apple Search Ads credentials, no scraping, no paid APIs,' covering 30 storefronts including markets ASA does not serve. Scoring code is public at github.com/respectlytics/respectaso (aso/scoring.py, aso/services.py). This is the closest thing to a validated open formula, though no published correlation study against ASA popularity exists — the vendor explicitly frames all industry numbers as estimates ('Nobody outside Apple' knows real volumes).
  src: https://github.com/respectlytics/respectaso
- [medium] Apple Search Ads covers only 91 markets versus 175 App Store storefronts, so roughly half of all storefronts have no popularity data at all — independent of any API access question.
  ASA expanded to 91 markets in December 2024 (per searchadsmaven.com as cited by RespectASO). Any ASA-passthrough tool structurally cannot serve popularity for the other ~84 storefronts, which disproportionately includes emerging markets where indie apps localize. Free observable-signal proxies (autocomplete, result counts, top-app strength) work on every storefront.
  src: https://respectaso.com/blog/apple-search-ads-popularity-unreliable-aso-keyword-data/
- [medium] Apple quietly launched a beta 'Monthly Search Term Rank Report' inside Apple Ads Insights using a different, non-comparable 1-100 monthly methodology that only surfaces keywords with a legacy popularity score of 35 or higher — hiding the long tail.
  Reported by APPlyzer (cited by RespectASO) as Apple's de facto replacement signal after the Oct 2025 collapse. Separately, the ASA custom-reports endpoint began returning 403 Forbidden in March 2026, discovered by developers in production (Apple developer forums) with no changelog. Net: Apple is actively reshaping which popularity surfaces exist in 2026, and the sanctioned ones are monthly-granularity, head-terms-only, and inside a paid ads account.
  src: https://applyzer.com/blog/apple-search-ads-popularity-drop-understanding-the-issue-and-how-to-stay-ahead
- [high] Apple's sanctioned popularity surfaces are the keyword suggestion tool inside an ASA account (5-dot popularity 'based on App Store searches') and the Recommendations page, which only produces keyword suggestions after campaigns have run.
  From Apple's own Keywords Best Practices page: 'You can also use the keyword suggestion tool in your account to find related terms. The tool displays keyword popularity based on App Store searches' and 'once your campaigns have had time to run, you can check the Recommendations page for keyword suggestions... relevant keywords that are being used in App Store searches for apps like yours.' Programmatic access requires an Apple Ads account, Account Admin-generated API user, OAuth 2, App Store Connect role gating, tax info, and a payment method — heavy friction that no AI agent can self-serve.
  src: https://ads.apple.com/app-store/best-practices/keywords
- [medium] Phiture's canonical keyword-optimization methodology names the iTunes Search API 'Priority' field as a second-best free popularity proxy alongside ASA Search Popularity, and no public study validating autocomplete suggestion ORDER against ASA popularity was found.
  Phiture: 'To date there is no equivalent to the Google Keyword Planner tool that shows average monthly searches... As a second best proxy, one can use either Priority returned by the iTunes API, Search Popularity Scores for Search Ads (ranging from 5-100) or other Search volume scores as predicted by any of the ASO tools.' Autocomplete suggestion presence/order is clearly load-bearing in commercial models (AppTweak's Play volume, the Apify actor's 'related searches'), but across all sources reviewed, no vendor or researcher has published a correlation study of autocomplete rank vs ASA popularity — it is an open validation gap.
  src: https://phiture.com/asostack/keyword-optimization-cycle-5f47388c2b5/
- [high] Appfigures' Apple Ads Intelligence demonstrates the competitor-keyword reverse-engineering pattern commercially: select competitor apps, see the keywords they bid on with popularity, organic rank, share-of-impressions, overlap, and 'not advertising' filters — including cross-country expansion with auto-translation.
  Their documented workflows: surface competitors' low-popularity long-tail keywords you aren't using, identify keywords where you rank high organically AND pay (to cut spend), and mine competitors' keywords in other storefronts. This is paid-side reverse-engineering; the organic-side equivalent (diffing a competitor's full ranked-keyword set against tokens visible in their title/subtitle to infer the hidden 100-char iOS keyword field) is implied by AppTweak's 'analyze which keywords competitors are targeting' but no vendor publishes the inference mechanics.
  src: https://appfigures.com/resources/guides/optimizing-your-apple-search-ads-campaign
