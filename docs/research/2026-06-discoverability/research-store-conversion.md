## SUMMARY
Store-listing conversion is decided almost entirely above the fold: SplitMetrics scroll-depth data shows 90% of iOS visitors see only the top 10% of a product page, which makes icon, title, rating stars, and the first 1-3 screenshots the dominant levers and the description's first lines nearly irrelevant on iOS. The quantified levers are: star rating (NP Digital's 49-company study gives a conversion index of 0.83 at 4.0 stars and 0.57 at 3.0 vs 1.00 at 5.0, with featuring effectively gated at 4.0-4.4+), screenshots (SplitMetrics' #1-ranked element; one screenshot change drove textPlus 119K installs), preview video (+15-25% per Adapty vs 20-40% per SplitMetrics — a real source conflict worth exposing as a range), and CPPs (+5.9-8.6% CVR, used by only 31% of apps). Category baselines vary 100x (Business 66.7% CVR vs board games 1.2%; Navigation >100%), and sources conflict across years (AppTweak 2020 said 33.7% average iOS CVR vs 25% in H1 2024), so any grading Sniffy ships must be category-relative, range-based, and source-attributed. The zero-budget indie playbook is concrete: Apple PPO (3 treatments, 90 days, 90% confidence, screenshots first) and Google Play Store Listing Experiments (free; official case studies show +20-45% installs), plus iOS in-app events — a free, searchable discovery surface (indexed 30-char event names, direct install from event cards, editorial pitch path) that almost no indie uses; Play promotional content showed +5% active users / +4% revenue in Google's beta. Critically for Sniffy, every high-value conversion signal here is estimable from public data: rating average/count (free iTunes API), ratings-count velocity as an install proxy, video presence and screenshot layout of top-10 category apps (scrape + vision LLM), event-card presence, and creative refresh cadence via screenshot-URL hashing over snapshots. Competitors (SplitMetrics, Storemaven, AppTweak) hold this intelligence in seat-licensed dashboards or agency engagements with no agent-accessible API or MCP, leaving the machine-readable, pay-per-request conversion-diagnosis niche open for Sniffy.

## FINDINGS
- [medium] Average page-view-to-install CVR is ~25% on the US App Store and ~27.3% on Google Play (AppTweak H1 2024 data), but spread by category is enormous: Business 66.7%, Weather 79.8%, Navigation 115%, vs board games at 1.2%.
  Adapty's benchmark roundup (sourced to AppTweak H1 2024) shows CVR is meaningless without subcategory context: Navigation exceeds 100% because most installs happen directly from search results without a page view (Apple counts first-time installs / page views). Other reference points: Photo & Video 60.8%, Productivity 59.7%, Health & Fitness 47.1%, Utilities 40.6%, Finance 32.8% (iOS) vs 19.7% (Play), Education 16.8% (iOS) vs 30.4% (Play), Social 13.1%, Racing games 20.6%. Platform direction flips per category, so iOS and Android need separate baselines.
  src: https://adapty.io/blog/app-store-conversion-rate/
- [medium] Funnel-stage benchmarks: impression-to-page-view 6-12%, page-view-to-install 25-27%, and iOS impression-to-install (Install Rate) only 3.6-3.8%.
  These are the three numbers Sniffy needs to grade a funnel: a CTR-like step (6-12%), an on-page conversion step (25-27%), and the blended Install Rate (3.6-3.8%, iOS only). Adapty also tabulates downstream subscription steps (download-to-trial 3.7-8.9%, trial-to-paid 38-54%) which matter for indie subscription apps. Note Install Rate can be far below CVR because most impressions never become page views.
  src: https://adapty.io/blog/app-store-conversion-rate/
- [high] Benchmark sources conflict materially across years and methodology: AppTweak's 2020 dataset put US iOS page-view-to-install at 33.7% (vs 25% in H1 2024), and Books category CVR is reported anywhere from 7.3% (AppTweak 2024) to 74.2% (Statista 2022).
  Business of Apps aggregates AppTweak, Storemaven and SplitMetrics data and the deltas are large: AppTweak 2020 says iOS 33.7% / Play 26.4% page-view-to-install; Storemaven's traffic-source split shows search-sourced impression-to-page-view of only ~2.9-5.9% by category while browse-sourced runs up to 55% and referrer-sourced up to 67.5%. Adapty itself flags the Books discrepancy as methodology/sampling variance. Implication for Sniffy: any benchmark grading must carry a source+year provenance label and ideally a range, not a point estimate — exactly matching Sniffy's existing provenance model.
  src: https://www.businessofapps.com/marketplace/app-store-optimization/research/app-store-optimization-statistics/
- [high] 90% of App Store visitors and 80% of Google Play visitors view only the top 10% of a product page (SplitMetrics scroll-depth data), so icon + title/subtitle + ratings + first 1-3 screenshots decide almost all conversions.
  Only ~15% of iOS and ~20% of Android visitors ever see the full page. This is the strongest evidence that description first-lines matter far less than the above-the-fold creative stack, and that screenshot order is a first-class conversion lever. Business of Apps also cites SplitMetrics decision-time data showing users decide in seconds (Photo & Video fell from 25s in 2018 to 8s in 2020).
  src: https://www.businessofapps.com/marketplace/app-store-optimization/research/app-store-optimization-statistics/
- [medium] Star rating maps to a steep conversion multiplier curve: relative to a 5.0-star app converting at index 1.00, 4.5 stars converts at 0.96, 4.0 at 0.83, 3.0 at 0.57, 2.0 at 0.15, and 1.0 at 0.08 (NP Digital study of 49 companies).
  The cliff between 4.0 and 3.0 stars (0.83 to 0.57, a 31% relative drop) and the collapse below 3.0 give Sniffy a quantitative way to convert a public rating average into an estimated CVR penalty. The same source reports featured apps are dominated by 4.4+ star ratings, and Adapty independently notes 90% of featured apps had 4.0+ — supporting a 'featuring eligibility' heuristic around the 4.0/4.4 thresholds.
  src: https://neilpatel.com/marketing-stats/app-downloads-vs-ratings/
- [high] SplitMetrics ranks creative impact, from thousands of tests, as: 1) screenshots, 2) app icon, 3) preview video — and its textPlus case shows a single screenshot change driving 119K+ incremental installs.
  The winning textPlus variation changed visual hierarchy only: bigger logo, higher-contrast benefit text ('Free unlimited texting & calling'), and a figure pose that guides the eye — no feature changes. SplitMetrics also cites Lab Cave's icon-iteration case (case-study slug reads '314-increase-in-installs', i.e. a ~31.4% install increase) and Hobnob, where an intuition-based rebrand reduced conversion — evidence that creative changes can be negative, which justifies testing before shipping. Screenshot order alone is called out as a conversion factor because 'most users only view the first few.'
  src: https://splitmetrics.com/blog/ab-testing/
- [medium] Reported app-preview-video conversion lift conflicts across sources: Adapty cites +15-25% CVR, while SplitMetrics (citing 2024-2025 Apple/ASO-platform data) cites 20-40% versus screenshots alone.
  Both agree direction (positive, strongest for complex/new-to-market apps) but ranges differ, so Sniffy should present video impact as a range with source attribution. A critical mechanical detail from SplitMetrics: iOS videos autoplay muted and sit first in the gallery, but a landscape video paired with portrait screenshots is demoted to the 'Closer Look' section where it has 'very little or no influence on CVR' — meaning video orientation mismatch is a detectable, gradeable defect. SplitMetrics also notes not every app benefits; for simple apps screenshots can suffice.
  src: https://splitmetrics.com/blog/create-app-preview-video-app-store-ios/
- [high] Apple Product Page Optimization (PPO) is a free, native A/B test: up to 3 treatments vs the original, max 90 days, configurable traffic split, results reported with 90% confidence in App Analytics, and treatments also surface in search results.
  Constraints that matter for an indie playbook: only one test at a time; new treatment assets must pass App Review; alternate icons must be included in the published binary; Apple's duration estimator uses your existing impressions/downloads to predict whether your desired CVR lift is detectable within 90 days — low-traffic apps may simply not reach significance with 3 treatments, so indies should run 1 treatment changing only the first screenshots. PPO is the zero-budget organic lever; it tests the default page, not paid traffic.
  src: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page-optimization/
- [medium] Custom Product Pages (CPPs) allow up to 35 alternate page variants with unique URLs for paid/referral traffic, are not organically indexed, and deliver reported CVR lifts of +5.9-8.6% — yet only 31% of apps and 26% of games use them.
  Adjust clarifies the PPO/CPP split: PPO = organic A/B testing of the default page; CPP = paid-traffic page variants attached to Apple Ads ad groups (keyword-intent matching, e.g. a 'Bike Trails' CPP for that keyword). Adapty's 2026 trends note Apple is adding CPP-keyword linking and that CPP underuse is one of the biggest untapped levers. Each CPP can change screenshots, previews, and 170-char promotional text (promo text is conversion-only, no keyword-ranking effect). For zero-budget indies CPPs only pay off if they have ads or external web/social traffic to route to the unique URLs.
  src: https://www.adjust.com/blog/custom-product-pages-app-store/
- [high] AppTweak's 2025 Apple Ads benchmarks (3,500 apps, 50K campaigns, $1B spend): median CPT $0.92, median CPI $1.80, and median tap-to-install conversion of 56% for search-results placements vs 15% Search Tab, 15% product pages, and 7.7% Today Tab.
  Apple's own stat (cited in the same article) is that ~65% of all App Store downloads happen directly after a search, which is why search-results placement converts so much better than discovery placements. US CPIs vary hugely by category (Sports $26.81, Games $12.28 vs cheaper Music/Utilities). These placement-level CRs give Sniffy plausible priors for what 'good' paid conversion looks like when grading an app's funnel or estimating the value of a keyword.
  src: https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-ads-benchmarks
- [high] Google Play Store Listing Experiments are free native A/B tests with official case studies showing Kongregate +45% installs, Tapps Games +20% installs, and Pincer Games +20% conversions.
  Google's own console page recommends testing one element at a time and running at least a week to absorb weekday/weekend traffic patterns, with automatic winning-variant detection and email alerts. This is the Android indie's zero-budget equivalent of PPO and is more flexible (text and graphics testable, localized experiments per country). The case-study lifts are vendor-published but first-party (Google).
  src: https://play.google.com/console/about/store-listing-experiments/
- [medium] Google Play promotional content (formerly LiveOps) drove +5% active users and +4% revenue on average for apps in Google's closed beta, and running frequent high-quality events is an explicit featuring-eligibility signal.
  Promotional content cards (offers, time-limited events, major updates) surface in the Games tab, Events tab, store listing, and search results, with deep links and no maximum number of concurrent events. AppTweak notes the 'Offer' type (discounts/trials) has its own Offers tab — something iOS in-app events lack. Category patterns are observable: Sports/Entertainment lean on time-limited events; Books/Education lean on major updates.
  src: https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/what-are-google-play-liveops-best-practices-examples
- [high] iOS In-App Events are a discovery surface, not just engagement: event cards appear in search results (replacing screenshots for existing users), are directly searchable by their 30-character indexed event name, allow direct app download from the card, and up to 10 can be live simultaneously.
  Apple's official page confirms events can be submitted independently of app versions (up to 15 approved in App Store Connect, 10 published at once, automatable via the App Store Connect API) and can be pitched to Apple's editorial team for featuring consideration — one of the few zero-budget paths to editorial placement. AppTweak's comparison adds that the event name is indexed for search, making events an extra keyword surface that most indie devs never use.
  src: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/in-app-events/
- [medium] Top-200 apps refresh screenshots 2-4 times per year (games up to 8x on Google Play), and 57% of top games A/B-tested screenshots at least twice in 2024 — creative staleness is itself a measurable competitive gap.
  Adapty's trends section quantifies metadata update cadence among category leaders, plus localization lift of +20-40% in local markets (recommended minimum 10 languages). Combined with the scroll-depth finding, this means 'when did this app last change its first 3 screenshots vs its top-10 competitors' is a legitimate, benchmarkable diagnosis signal that can be computed purely from periodic public-page snapshots.
  src: https://adapty.io/blog/app-store-conversion-rate/

## OPPORTUNITIES
- (free-data) Ship an `estimatedConversionIndex` field (provenance: `inferred`) that multiplies the NP Digital star-rating curve (4.0=0.83, 3.0=0.57, ...) against the app's category CVR baseline, returning an estimated CVR range plus the dollar-equivalent penalty vs a 4.5-star peer.
  WHY: SplitMetrics/Storemaven only give you real CVR after you connect App Store Connect and pay for a seat; AppTweak's CVR benchmarks live behind a dashboard. No competitor exposes a machine-readable 'your rating is costing you ~X% of installs' estimate an agent can act on in one API call.
  DATA: Rating average + count from the free iTunes Lookup API (already in Sniffy's shallowScan), plus a static benchmark table (category CVR ranges + star multiplier curve) baked into the report version. All free.
- (vision-llm) Creative-stack gap audit: for the app's category/top keyword, scrape the top-10 ranked apps' public listing pages and compute video presence rate, screenshot count distribution, portrait-vs-panoramic layout, caption presence, and first-screenshot message style — then flag where the audited app deviates from category leaders.
  WHY: Since 90% of visitors see only the top 10% of the page, the first-impression frame is where conversion is won, yet keyword tools barely touch creative. SplitMetrics sells this as agency work; AppTweak's creative intelligence is an enterprise dashboard add-on. A structured JSON 'your category's top 10: 8/10 have videos, you don't' is uniquely agent-consumable.
  DATA: Public App Store web pages / iTunes Lookup screenshotUrls for top-ranked apps (Sniffy already ranks them); a vision LLM pass for caption/message-style classification. Video presence requires page scrape (not in Lookup API).
- (free-data) Ratings-velocity install proxy: snapshot userRatingCount per (app, country) on Sniffy's existing rank-history cadence and expose ratings-count delta (weekly) as an install-momentum signal with provenance `inferred`, benchmarked against the category's top-10 median velocity.
  WHY: Install estimates are the most-paywalled number in ASO (Sensor Tower/data.ai charge thousands per month, dashboard-only). Ratings velocity is a free, defensible proxy — roughly proportional to installs at a category-stable review rate — and Sniffy already has the snapshot infrastructure from rank history.
  DATA: iTunes Lookup ratingCount over time (free); Sniffy's existing Redis history store. Zero marginal cost beyond cron snapshots.
- (free-data) Zero-budget experiment planner in the paid diagnosis: given estimated impressions (from rank + the 6-12% impression-to-page-view and 3.6-3.8% install-rate benchmarks), tell the founder whether an Apple PPO test can reach 90% confidence within the 90-day cap, and prescribe the highest-leverage single test (first screenshot > icon > video, per SplitMetrics priority) with concrete treatment hypotheses.
  WHY: Apple's own duration estimator requires you to already be in App Store Connect; no API tool tells a low-traffic indie 'you don't have enough impressions for 3 treatments — run 1 treatment, change only screenshots 1-2.' This turns benchmark research into an actionable, agent-executable plan.
  DATA: Sniffy's existing rank data + static benchmark constants + standard sample-size math. Free.
- (free-data) In-app events / promotional content detector: scrape the public product page for live iOS event cards and Play promotional content, report presence/absence for the audited app and its competitor trail, and flag the unused free discovery surface (indexed 30-char event names on iOS; featuring-eligibility signal on Play).
  WHY: AppTweak markets in-app-event tracking as an exclusive dashboard feature; nobody exposes it via API or MCP. For indies it is a literally free visibility channel (events submit independently of app versions) that almost no small app uses — a high-credibility 'best next sniff' recommendation.
  DATA: Public App Store / Play web page scrape (event cards render server-side); no auth needed. Free but needs scraper selectors maintained against page drift — fits Sniffy's existing schema-drift monitoring.
- (free-data) Creative staleness score: hash the screenshot URL set per snapshot and report 'days since last creative refresh' for the app vs its top-10 competitors, graded against the top-200 norm of 2-4 refreshes/year (8x for Play games).
  WHY: Update-cadence intelligence is an enterprise feature (AppTweak timeline view) and never exposed programmatically. Screenshot URLs change when assets change, so staleness is detectable from snapshots Sniffy already takes — turning a passive cache into a differentiated longitudinal signal that compounds with hosting time (the 'commercial host' moat).
  DATA: iTunes Lookup screenshotUrls hashed per snapshot over time; Play listing scrape equivalent. Free, but value accrues with snapshot history.
- (free-data) Benchmark provenance ranges instead of point estimates: every benchmark-graded field (CVR, rating threshold, video lift) returns {low, high, source, year} — e.g. video lift reported as 15-40% citing both Adapty and SplitMetrics — so agents can reason about uncertainty.
  WHY: Competitor blogs publish conflicting numbers (33.7% vs 25% CVR; 7.3% vs 74.2% for Books) with no machine-readable reconciliation. Sniffy's existing provenance system extends naturally to benchmark citations, and 'honest ranges with sources' is exactly what an LLM agent needs to avoid hallucinating certainty for its human.
  DATA: Curated static benchmark corpus (extends the @gosniffy/aso-knowledge pattern already in the repo) with source URLs and years. Free, maintenance is editorial.
