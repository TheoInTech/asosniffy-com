## SUMMARY
Ratings/reviews act on discoverability through two distinct mechanisms: a persistent cross-query quality signal (average + volume) and a behavioral freshness signal (review velocity), and 2024-2026 algorithm rebalancing has shifted weight toward the latter — industry analyses claim 4.2 stars with ~100 fresh reviews/week outranks 4.5 with 5/week. Key thresholds: below ~3.5 visibility is actively suppressed, 4.0 is the credibility floor shown in search results, and top-3 positions for competitive keywords cluster above 4.5; volume compounds the signal (4.6 from 12k ratings beats 4.8 from 200). Conversion uplift numbers conflict across sources (3→4 stars = +89-92% per Apptentive-derived posts vs Storemaven's +30-35% for 3.5→4.5 over 100M visitors), so Sniffy should present bands, not point estimates. Mechanics are well documented from official sources: Apple's summary rating is per-territory and resettable per version (written reviews persist), SKStoreReviewController allows 3 prompts per 365 days, Google Play's displayed rating is officially weighted toward recent ratings, is localized per registered country since Nov 2021, holds new reviews ~24h for fraud screening, and enforces an undisclosed time-bound in-app-review quota. Developer replies trigger push+email notifications and Play Console ships native "rating change with vs without reply" analytics; AppFollow claims Google confirmed responsiveness as a direct Play ranking signal (vendor claim). Apple now generates LLM review summaries on product pages (iOS 18.4+, weekly refresh) — an entirely new surface no ASO tool optimizes for yet. The competitive landscape (Appbot $49-479+/mo seat-licensed with API as a paid add-on at $166+/mo; AppFollow free tier but dashboard/CRM-oriented) is uniformly not agent-payable per-request, leaving competitor negative-review mining and velocity-gap signals wide open for Sniffy. Public data access is feasible: Sniffy's existing iTunes RSS (~500 reviews, mostrecent) covers iOS, and google-play-scraper-style continuation-token pagination reliably returns Play written reviews (including reply fields) per locale without auth — silent star-only ratings are not retrievable.

## FINDINGS
- [medium] Review velocity often beats absolute rating as a ranking input: an app with 4.2 stars and ~100 fresh reviews/week typically outranks one with 4.5 stars and 5 reviews/week.
  App Radar's 2026 ranking-factors guide (16 factors) lists 'Ratings and reviews' as high-impact with the explicit note 'velocity often beats absolute rating', and reports both stores rebalanced 2024-2026 away from pre-install signals (keywords, ratings, raw downloads) toward post-install signals (retention, conversion, semantic relevance). Both stores read average, count, recency, and sentiment. This is vendor analysis across thousands of apps, not an official disclosure.
  src: https://appradar.com/academy/app-store-ranking-factors
- [medium] Rating thresholds: below ~3.5 stars search visibility is measurably suppressed, 4.0 is the key credibility threshold, and apps holding top-3 positions for competitive keywords almost universally maintain >4.5.
  AppFollow's 2026 ranking-factors guide states apps under 3.5 see 'measurably reduced search visibility across the board' (treated as a quality flag overriding metadata/velocity), correlation between rating and keyword position strengthens progressively above 4.0, and volume compounds the signal: 'a 4.8 rating from 200 reviews carries less algorithmic weight than a 4.6 from 12,000 reviews'. Ratings are described as a persistent cross-query quality signal, unlike conversion rate which is query-specific.
  src: https://appfollow.io/blog/aso-ranking-factors
- [high] Apple officially confirms the summary rating is specific to each App Store territory and can be reset when releasing a new version; written reviews are NOT reset and continue to display.
  Apple's developer page states the summary rating 'is specific to each territory' and 'you can reset it when you release a new version', while warning to use the feature sparingly because few ratings discourage downloads. Resetting never removes written reviews. This makes a 'reset advisor' computable: compare current-version vs lifetime rating per territory and recommend reset only when an update fixed the complained-about issues.
  src: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/ratings-and-reviews/
- [high] SKStoreReviewController allows at most 3 rating prompts per 365-day period per app; Google Play's in-app review API enforces an undisclosed, time-bound per-user quota that can silently suppress the dialog.
  Apple's official page: 'You can prompt for ratings up to three times in a 365-day period.' Google's Play Core docs: 'Google Play enforces a time-bound quota on how often a user can be shown the review dialog... the quota is subject to change', launchReviewFlow gives no guarantee or error when the dialog is skipped, and apps must not gate flows on it. Net effect: prompt timing (post aha-moment) is a scarce budgeted resource on both stores, which makes a quota-aware prompting plan a legitimate paid-report recommendation.
  src: https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/in-app-review
- [high] Google officially states the rating users see on Google Play is weighted toward more recent ratings, and new ratings/reviews are held back ~24 hours for fraud screening before affecting the published rating.
  Play Console Help: 'the rating that users see on Google Play is weighted towards more recent ratings to reflect changes and updates' — Console separately exposes 'Google Play rating' (recent-weighted) vs 'Lifetime average rating'. New submissions 'are generally held back for around 24 hours' for suspicious-activity detection, though developers can see and reply immediately. This means a recovering app's displayed Play rating can climb well above its lifetime average — a concrete, free-to-compute signal (displayed vs lifetime delta).
  src: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/138230?hl=en
- [high] Google Play ratings have been localized per user's registered country since November 2021 and split per device type (phone/tablet/Chromebook/wearable) since early 2022, matching iOS's per-storefront model.
  Google announced (Aug 2021) that users see the rating from their registered country starting Nov 2021, with device-type splits in early 2022. Google committed to 10 weeks advance notice for developers whose rating would shift more than 0.2 in any market representing >5% of pageviews. Consequence: per-country rating health is a real localization signal on BOTH stores now, so a single global rating in a report is misleading.
  src: https://www.yellowhead.com/blog/google-play-ratings-and-reviews-will-soon-be-more-localized-than-ever/
- [high] Developer replies have official, measurable feedback loops: Play notifies reviewers by push + email, allows one editable public reply per review, and Play Console natively reports rating changes 'with replies' vs 'without replies'.
  Play Console Help documents the reply notification flow, suggested replies (English-only), the Reply to Reviews API (with quotas and a quota-increase form, used by Zendesk-style integrations), and an analytics view showing whether users increased/decreased/kept their rating after a reply vs without one — i.e., Google itself ships reply-ROI measurement. Apple's page confirms reviewers are notified and 'has the option to update their review', and recommends prioritizing lowest-star reviews and replying after major releases.
  src: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/138230?hl=en
- [medium] AppFollow claims Google Play has confirmed developer responsiveness (whether/how fast devs respond, plus review sentiment trajectory) as a direct Play ranking signal, while on iOS reply impact is indirect via conversion.
  AppFollow's ranking-factors guide: 'Google Play confirmed developer responsiveness as a direct Google Play Store ranking signal. The Play algorithm factors in whether developers respond to reviews, how quickly they respond, and the sentiment trajectory of reviews over time.' For iOS it frames responses as a conversion signal (users read responses pre-install). This is a vendor assertion without a linked primary Google statement — treat as directional, but it is consistent with Play Console's native reply-impact analytics.
  src: https://appfollow.io/blog/aso-ranking-factors
- [medium] Star-band conversion uplift figures conflict across sources: 3→4 stars is claimed at +89-92% (Apptentive-derived) but Storemaven's 100M-visitor analysis found 3.5→4.5 lifts install conversion only +30-35% on average.
  Arkenea (citing Apptentive) claims 2→3 stars = +306% and 3→4 = +92% conversion; mobile-marketing-masterclass cites +280% for 2→3 and Apptentive's '46% of users stay away from apps rated worse than 4 stars'; Strataigize cites Storemaven's analysis of 100M+ store visitors at +30-35% for 3.5→4.5 (60%+ in outlier categories). The Apptentive numbers are stated-preference survey-era figures (pre-2020 origin, frequently recycled); Storemaven's is observed behavior. Sniffy should report ranges with provenance rather than a single multiplier.
  src: https://www.strataigize.com/blog/app-store-rating-strategy
- [medium] Cross-domain evidence says conversion does NOT increase monotonically with rating: PowerReviews (20M+ e-commerce product pages) found 4.75-4.99 is the conversion sweet spot while flat 5.0 converts no better than 3.0-3.49.
  PowerReviews' benchmark: biggest band-to-band conversion jumps are 3.0-3.49→3.5-3.99 (+20%) and 3.5-3.99→4.0-4.24 (+19%); 5.0-star products convert like 3.0-3.49 products (too-good-to-be-true distrust); 62.4% of shoppers actively click into 1-star reviews and still convert 108.8% higher than non-interactors. Caveat: this is e-commerce, not app stores — but it is the strongest quantified evidence that 'perfect 5.0 with few reviews' is a conversion liability, which supports flagging low-volume-perfect-rating apps in a report.
  src: https://www.powerreviews.com/average-rating-impact-on-conversion/
- [high] Apple now auto-generates LLM review summaries on App Store product pages (iOS/iPadOS 18.4+), refreshed at least weekly, phased rollout starting US English — a new conversion surface composed entirely from review content.
  Apple's official page: summaries 'compile highlights and key information from individual user reviews into a short paragraph', refresh 'at least once a week' for apps with enough reviews, expanding to more storefronts/languages over the year; developers can only 'report a concern', not edit. This means the dominant themes in recent reviews now get rewritten into Apple's own product-page copy — negative theme clusters become literal listing text, raising the stakes of review mining.
  src: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/ratings-and-reviews/
- [high] Appbot prices review sentiment/topic intelligence at $49-479+/month, seat- and source-limited, with API access only as an optional paid add-on starting at the ~$166+/mo Large tier.
  Plans page: Small $49/mo annual ($59 monthly, 1 user, 5 tracked apps), Medium $99/mo ($119, 3 users, 40 apps), Large from $166/mo ($219, 7+ users, 100+ apps, 'Optional API Access'), XL from $479/mo annual-only (20+ users). All tiers include sentiment, topics/custom topics, all-countries fetch, cross-source comparison and benchmarks; automated translations are quota'd 300/1,500/3,000 per month. Competitor apps count against the same 'sources' quota. Nothing is per-request or crypto/agent-payable; the cheapest API path is ~$2,000+/year.
  src: https://appbot.co/plans/
- [medium] AppFollow differentiates on LLM-based review intelligence — AI semantic tags, AI review summaries, reply-effect tracking, knowledge-base-trained AI replies, and an autonomous reply agent 'coming 2026' — with a free plan and full API access by plan.
  AppFollow's comparison page (vendor-biased but feature-specific) lists: free plan with unlimited owned apps, AI semantic tags + custom AI tags vs Appbot's keyword-matching NLP, 'reply effect tracking' that measures reply impact on star ratings, AI auto-replies with template rephrasing/translation, competitor intelligence ('analyze competitor review sentiment, feature requests, bug reports... to find market gaps') marked full vs Appbot 'Limited', and full API access included by plan vs Appbot's paid add-on. The whole stack is dashboard/CRM seat-licensed — no per-request purchase path an agent could pay autonomously.
  src: https://appfollow.io/compare-appfollow-vs-appbot
- [high] Google Play written reviews remain reliably scrapable from public surfaces in 2025-2026 via continuation-token pagination (google-play-scraper), but only reviews with text are retrievable — silent star-only ratings are not.
  The actively maintained JoMingyu/google-play-scraper exposes reviews()/reviews_all() with continuation tokens, sort (NEWEST etc.), and per lang/country ISO params; the JS equivalent Sniffy already vendors (_gplay.ts) works the same way. The well-known count mismatch (e.g., 69,600 'reviews' on the store page vs 7,608 scraped) is explained: the store counts all ratings while the public feed only returns written reviews — confirmed by manual scrolling matching scraper output. Per-locale fetches are independent, so multi-country coverage requires one paginated pass per (lang, country).
  src: https://github.com/JoMingyu/google-play-scraper
- [medium] Conversion-focused custom product pages adoption data underlines how under-exploited conversion levers are: Apple CPP adopters saw search conversion climb from 42% to 56%, yet only 31% of apps used CPPs as of Q1 2025.
  App Radar's 2026 guide cites 6.56B impressions through custom product pages with conversion rising 42%→56% among adopters and only 31% adoption — context for why Sniffy's conversion-side recommendations (ratings above the fold: icon + title + rating + first screenshot form 'the complete conversion unit' in search results) have headroom. A 4.0+ rating is visible directly in search results before any listing copy is read.
  src: https://appradar.com/academy/app-store-ranking-factors

## OPPORTUNITIES
- (free-data) Review velocity + recency-delta signal: compute reviews/week trend, displayed-vs-lifetime rating delta (Play), and current-version-vs-lifetime delta (iOS) for the target app AND each competitor, and flag the 'velocity gap' (e.g., 'competitor X earns 38 reviews/wk vs your 4 — velocity beats your 0.3-star rating edge').
  WHY: Velocity is the signal both stores now reward most (recent-weighted Play rating is official; 'velocity beats absolute rating' is the consensus 2026 analyst view), yet Appbot/AppFollow only show it in seat-licensed dashboards — no competitor sells a per-request, agent-payable velocity comparison. Sniffy already fetches timestamped iOS reviews via RSS, so this is pure derived math on data it already has.
  DATA: Already free: iTunes RSS review timestamps (mostrecent sort is ideal for velocity), iTunes Lookup current-version vs lifetime rating fields, gplay app payload (recent-weighted score + total ratings). Competitor velocity needs the same RSS/gplay calls per competitor app — same providers, more cache keys.
- (cheap-api) Competitor negative-review positioning-gap miner: pull each competitor's recent 1-2 star reviews, LLM-cluster complaint themes, and emit 'positioning gaps' — recurring complaints the target app could own as differentiators, with ready-to-paste subtitle/screenshot-caption copy (paid-only section).
  WHY: AppFollow gates 'competitor intelligence' behind upper dashboard plans and Appbot counts competitors against source quotas ($49-166+/mo, API as paid add-on) — none of it is buyable per-request by an agent. Sniffy can sell exactly one competitor-gap report for cents over x402, and its existing AI-synthesis step makes LLM clustering strictly better than its current heuristic token sentiment. Fits the existing 'competitor trail' framing.
  DATA: Free: competitors' iOS reviews via existing RSS provider (cap ~500/app, fine for theme mining) and Play written reviews via existing _gplay.ts with continuation tokens. Plus one LLM clustering call per competitor (cheap, cacheable by reportVersion).
- (free-data) Rating-reset advisor (iOS) and quota-aware prompting plan: recommend per-territory whether to reset the summary rating on the next release (only when current-version rating materially exceeds the territory summary AND volume is sufficient), plus a concrete prompt budget plan around SKStoreReviewController's 3-per-365-days limit and Play's undisclosed quota.
  WHY: The reset decision is per-territory and high-stakes (Apple warns 'use sparingly' — a reset with thin new volume tanks conversion), and no tool computes the recommendation from data; ASO agencies do it manually. The prompting plan is pure knowledge-corpus content for @gosniffy/aso-knowledge with official citations, making the free MCP server more authoritative.
  DATA: Free: iTunes Lookup averageUserRating/userRatingCount vs current-version equivalents per storefront (already fetched per-country for localization analysis); Apple + Android official doc citations for the knowledge corpus.

  WHY: Brand-new surface (phased rollout 2025-2026, weekly refresh, developer cannot edit it) that no ASO tool optimizes for yet — being early gives Sniffy a citable, demo-friendly 'Apple will tell users X about your app' signal. It reuses the same review corpus and LLM pass as the gap miner, so marginal cost is near zero.
  DATA: Free: existing iOS RSS reviews (mostrecent matches Apple's freshness bias); one LLM summarization call. Output labeled 'inferred' provenance since Apple's actual summary isn't programmatically retrievable.
