## SUMMARY
Apple now officially describes App Store search ranking as two signal classes: textual relevance (title, subtitle, keyword field, primary category) and behavioral relevance (downloads, ratings/reviews, engagement) — and as of February 2026 it has published a research paper confirming the production ranker is augmented with LLM-generated semantic relevance labels from a fine-tuned 3B-parameter model, which lifted conversion +0.24% worldwide and helped most on tail queries where behavioral data is sparse. The biggest 2025 mechanical change is that screenshot captions are OCR-indexed since ~June 2025 (empirically verified by Appfigures across thousands of keywords), and custom product pages rank organically for assigned keywords since July 2025. WWDC 2025 introduced LLM-generated, human-reviewed App Store Tags that appear in search results and are developer-editable in App Store Connect — a concept layer on top of literal keywords. Descriptions and promotional text remain NOT indexed per Apple and Appfigures, though one 2026 industry guide (AppRadar) claims otherwise — a live conflict worth tracking. Match mechanics are well documented: words (not phrases) are tokenized and combined across title/subtitle/keyword field within a single localization, plurals are stemmed as duplicates, compound title words split on capital letters, and each storefront indexes multiple locales' metadata (the US indexes 10), making cross-localization the cheapest keyword-space expansion available. Behavioral weights are not published, but industry reverse-engineering converges on download velocity (relative to category baseline) as the strongest earned signal, with conversion rate, review velocity (which often beats absolute rating), and retention (D1>35%, D7>15% healthy bands) close behind. For Sniffy, the highest-leverage measurable-from-outside signals competitors underserve are: screenshot caption OCR audits, AI tag tracking, cross-localization gap arithmetic, token-combination simulation, and tail-query semantic coverage scoring — all on free public data and all absent or dashboard-locked in incumbent tools.

## FINDINGS
- [high] Apple officially names exactly two ranking signal classes for App Store search: text relevance (title, subtitle, keywords, primary category) and user behavior (downloads, ratings and reviews, 'and more').
  Apple's developer page states: 'Search results are based on a number of factors, including text relevance (matches for your app's title, subtitle, keywords, and primary category), as well as user behavior (downloads, ratings and reviews, and more).' It also confirms primary AND secondary categories are indexed by the search algorithm, and that search results may include in-app events, promoted in-app purchases, custom product pages, and LLM-generated app tags. This is the canonical first-party statement of what is indexed.
  src: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/
- [high] Apple's production App Store ranker is now augmented with LLM-generated semantic relevance labels — a February 2026 Apple research paper confirms a worldwide A/B test showing +0.24% conversion rate lift, concentrated in tail queries.
  The paper 'Scaling Search Relevance: Augmenting App Store Ranking with LLM-Generated Judgments' (Apple Machine Learning Research, published Feb 2026, arXiv:2602.23234) describes fine-tuning a specialized ~3B-parameter LLM on human relevance judgments, finding it outperformed a much larger pre-trained model, then generating millions of textual relevance labels to augment the production ranker. The worldwide A/B test showed a statistically significant +0.24% conversion increase, with 'the most substantial performance gains occurring in tail queries, where the new textual relevance labels provide a robust signal in the absence of reliable behavioral relevance labels.' Implication: semantic fit now competes with literal keyword match, especially on long-tail queries — exactly where indie apps compete.
  src: https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/augmenting-app
- [high] Since ~June 2025, Apple OCR-indexes screenshot caption text and treats it as part of keyword metadata — empirically verified by Appfigures across thousands of keywords.
  Appfigures' Ariel Michaeli (June 16, 2025) analyzed thousands of keywords after a major rank shake-up and concluded Apple extracts visible text from screenshot captions and uses it for ranking. Key mechanics: caption keywords do NOT compete with name/subtitle/keyword-field keywords (repetition in screenshots reinforces core keywords rather than triggering the duplicate penalty); apps were observed ranking for keywords appearing ONLY in screenshots, though as a weaker signal; OCR legibility matters (high contrast, legible fonts, captions likely read from top/bottom zones); app preview videos do not appear to be read yet. AppTweak and AppRadar independently corroborate the June 2025 timing; Apple has not officially confirmed.
  src: https://appfigures.com/resources/guides/app-store-algorithm-update-2025
- [high] WWDC 2025 introduced App Store Tags: LLM-generated, human-reviewed concept labels derived from app metadata, descriptions, and screenshots, shown in search results and manageable by developers in App Store Connect.
  TechCrunch (June 11, 2025) reported the WWDC25 App Store Connect session: tags are generated by Apple's LLMs from sources including description, category, metadata, and screenshots, then human-reviewed; users can tap tags to reach curated collections of apps with similar functionality; developers get final say and can deselect tags in App Store Connect. Apple's own search page confirms tags 'are generated using large language models (LLMs) based on the metadata you've provided in App Store Connect' and appear in search results. This is a concept/feature layer alongside — not yet replacing — literal keywords.
  src: https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/11/at-wwdc-apple-says-it-will-use-ai-to-tag-apps-to-improve-discoverability-on-the-app-store/
- [high] Custom Product Pages became an organic search ranking surface in July 2025: developers assign keywords to CPPs (per localization), the matching CPP replaces the default page in search results, and keyword-only CPP updates skip app review.
  AppTweak (July 2025) documents that WWDC25 added keyword assignment to CPPs, and in July 2025 Apple enabled CPPs to appear in organic App Store search results when the app ranks for an assigned keyword. Distinct keywords can be assigned per localization of a single CPP (e.g., 'home workout' US vs 'gym tracker' UK on the same CPP). Apple's developer search page confirms: 'Enable your custom product pages to appear in search results for those selected keywords... each keyword combination is unique to a single product page.' AppRadar adds adoption data: only ~31% of apps used CPPs as of Q1 2025, with adopter conversion rates climbing from 42% to 56%.
  src: https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/app-store-ranking-factors
- [high] Apple explicitly states promotional text does NOT affect search ranking, and Appfigures finds descriptions still not indexed — directly conflicting with AppRadar's 2026 guide that lists both as indexed.
  Apple's developer search page: 'keep in mind that promotional text doesn't affect your app's search ranking so it should not be used to display keywords.' Appfigures (June 2025): 'I haven't found any indication that app descriptions are being indexed. Historically, Apple stayed away from the description, and that seems to be the case still.' Conflict: AppRadar's 2026 ranking-factors table claims Promotional Text is '170 chars, indexed' (Medium weight) and Long Description is 'indexed at lower weight.' The Apple primary source and Appfigures' empirical work should win, but the LLM-relevance era blurs this — Apple's tag-generation LLM does read descriptions, so description text can influence tags/semantic matching without being classically keyword-indexed.
  src: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/
- [high] Apple's keyword field rules confirm plural stemming and cross-field deduplication: plurals count as duplicates, words already in title/subtitle/category are wasted, and spaces are allowed within multi-word keyword phrases.
  Apple's developer page on the 100-character keyword field: avoid 'plurals of words you've already included, such as climbs and climb, as these are considered duplicates'; 'don't repeat any words included in your app name, subtitle, or category'; format is comma-separated with no spaces after commas but 'you can use spaces to separate words within keyword phrases. For example: Property,House,Real Estate'; generic terms ('app', 'game') and filler words add no value. This is first-party confirmation of stemming behavior and the no-repetition rule that ASO tools encode heuristically.
  src: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/
- [high] Each App Store territory indexes metadata from a primary locale plus several secondary locales — the US storefront indexes 10 localizations, giving up to 10x the keyword character space via cross-localization.
  MobileAction (April 2026) and aso.dev both document territory-level keyword indexation: the US indexes EN_US (primary) plus Arabic, Chinese Simplified, Chinese Traditional, French, Korean, Portuguese (Brazil), Russian, Spanish (Mexico), and Vietnamese as secondary locales. Keywords placed in any of those locales' title/subtitle/keyword fields rank in the US, even filled with English words. Additionally, an app's primary localization is indexed in ALL countries. This is the documented basis for the classic 'borrow es-MX keywords for the US' trick. Not officially published by Apple as a ranking doc, but the territory-locale table is derived from Apple's own localization support matrix and is consistently reproduced across major ASO vendors.
  src: https://www.mobileaction.co/blog/app-store-cross-localization/
- [medium] Apple indexes individual tokens, not literal phrases: words from title, subtitle, and keyword field are combined into phrase permutations — but only within a single localization, never across locales.
  aso.dev's cross-localization guide documents the match mechanics: 'The App Store does not index phrases as-is — it combines words from the Title, Subtitle, and Keywords fields' (so 'best,photo,editor,iPhone' yields 'best photo', 'photo editor', 'editor for iPhone', etc.); each word is counted only once even if repeated across fields or localizations; phrases form only within one locale (Title 'Apple' in EN-US + Subtitle 'Music' in EN-GB will NOT index 'Apple Music'); compound words in the Title split on capital letters ('AppStoreConnect' indexes as 'app', 'store', 'connect', 'appstoreconnect' — the camelCase trick); some words ('app', 'game', 'for') are auto-indexed for all apps and waste space; words earlier in the Title carry higher priority.
  src: https://aso.dev/metadata/cross-localization/
- [medium] Industry reverse-engineering converges on download velocity (relative to category baseline) as the strongest earned ranking signal, with conversion rate, review velocity, and retention close behind — and review velocity often beats absolute rating.
  AppRadar/SplitMetrics' 2026 ranking-factors guide (based on 'thousands of apps') weights the five off-metadata signals: download velocity (Highest), conversion rate from impressions (High), ratings/reviews where 'velocity often beats absolute rating — an app with 4.2 stars and 100 fresh reviews per week typically outranks an app with 4.5 stars and 5 reviews per week' (High), retention + quality with healthy bands of D1 > 35% and D7 > 15% (High), and update cadence (Medium). They also claim both stores rebalanced 2024-2026 away from pre-install signals (keyword density, raw downloads, star rating) toward post-install signals (retention curves, CVR, review sentiment, semantic relevance). No official weights exist; treat as practitioner consensus, not ground truth.
  src: https://appradar.com/academy/app-store-ranking-factors
- [medium] Apps rated under 3.5 stars see significantly reduced search visibility, while ratings above 4.0 correlate with higher keyword rankings.
  AppTweak's July 2025 ranking-factors guide states these thresholds explicitly as observed correlations. Combined with Apple's own statement that ratings and reviews 'can influence how your app ranks in App Store search,' the rating signal is one of the few behavioral factors that is both officially confirmed and fully measurable from public data (rating value, count, and recency are all on the public listing).
  src: https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/app-store-ranking-factors
- [medium] In-app events are search-indexed via their event name and short description (not long description), and promoted in-app purchase names are indexed at low weight (64 chars per IAP).
  AppRadar's 2026 factor table: 'In-App Events: event name + short description indexed; long description not' (Medium weight) and 'In-App Purchase names: 64 chars per IAP, indexed' (Low weight, Apple-only). Apple's developer search page corroborates the surfaces: in-app events appear in search results (existing users see the event card; new users see screenshots), and promoted in-app purchases appear in search results and are tappable. AppTweak confirms 'Apple indexes in-app events and considers their relevance to user queries.' These are extra indexable surfaces most indie developers leave empty.
  src: https://appradar.com/academy/app-store-ranking-factors
- [low] In March 2026 Apple reportedly inserted additional paid ad placements between organic search results, compressing the organic window — with ~65% of downloads attributed to search.
  AppRadar's 2026 guide states: 'Around 65% of app downloads come through search. In March 2026 Apple compressed the organic window further by adding paid ad placements between organic results.' Single-source claim not yet corroborated by Apple or a second vendor in this research pass, so confidence is low — but if true it raises the stakes on every organic position and on Apple Ads spend as an indirect velocity lever (AppTweak also notes Apple Ads grow install velocity, which feeds back into organic rank).
  src: https://appradar.com/academy/app-store-ranking-factors
- [high] The split between externally measurable and owner-only ranking inputs is clean: metadata, ranks, ratings, reviews, tags, screenshots, update cadence, and in-app events are public; impressions, conversion rate, retention, source-type attribution, and the keyword field's literal contents are App Store Connect-only.
  Apple directs developers to App Analytics in App Store Connect for 'impressions, conversion rate, downloads' filterable by source type — these never appear publicly. The 100-char keyword field is explicitly hidden ('not visible to users' per AppTweak) and can only be reverse-inferred from observed rank positions. Conversely, everything else that drives textual relevance is scrapeable: title/subtitle per locale (iTunes lookup), category, screenshot captions (via OCR — now ranking-relevant), AI tags (visible in search results), in-app events, CPP variants (visible per keyword search), rating value/count/recency, review text/velocity, and release-note cadence. Download velocity sits in between: owner-truth in ASC, third-party panel estimates (Appfigures, Sensor Tower) outside.
  src: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/

## OPPORTUNITIES
- (vision-llm) Screenshot caption OCR audit: extract caption text from the app's and competitors' public screenshots, score keyword alignment with title/subtitle, flag passive captions ('Easy to Use') vs active keyword captions, and check OCR-readability (contrast, font size, caption placement).
  WHY: Screenshot indexing is the single biggest algorithm change of 2025 and most ASO tools still treat screenshots purely as conversion assets; Appfigures gates caption analysis behind dashboard seats with no API. Sniffy can ship it as a provenance-labeled section in the paid diagnose, fully agent-consumable, and the recommendation output ('rewrite caption 2 to: AI Photo Editor') is exactly the ready-to-paste content indie hackers pay for.
  DATA: Screenshot URLs are free via the public iTunes lookup/app page; caption extraction needs OCR (tesseract, free) or a vision LLM call per screenshot set (~10 images).
- (free-data) AI tag intelligence: track the new LLM-generated App Store Tags shown on search results and product pages, diff the target app's tag set against category leaders, and recommend metadata/description changes likely to earn missing high-traffic tags.
  WHY: Tags are the concept layer Apple is building search around (WWDC25), they are publicly visible, and almost no incumbent tool tracked them as of mid-2025 (AppTweak only suggests 'drawing inspiration' manually). Because tags are LLM-generated from metadata including the description, this also resurrects the description as an optimization surface — a non-obvious insight competitors miss.
  DATA: Tags render in public App Store search results and product pages — free via scraping; needs storefront-aware scraping since tags vary by locale.
- (free-data) Cross-localization gap report: using the static territory-to-indexed-locales table, compute exactly how many indexed-but-empty keyword fields the app is wasting per target country (e.g., 9 unused 100-char fields + 9 title/subtitle pairs in the US) and generate fill-ready keyword sets per borrowed locale.
  WHY: This is the highest-leverage free keyword-space expansion on iOS and it is pure arithmetic on public data — yet tools like MobileAction and AppTweak only explain the concept in blog posts or bury locale coverage in enterprise dashboards. Sniffy already ships multi-storefront localization gap analysis (franc), so this extends an existing module rather than building new infrastructure, and the output is mechanically actionable by a coding agent updating App Store Connect.
  DATA: Static territory/locale mapping (free, from Apple's localization matrix as reproduced by MobileAction/aso.dev) + per-locale metadata via free iTunes lookup API.
- (free-data) Token-combination simulator: tokenize title + subtitle (+ inferred keyword-field candidates) per locale and compute the full set of indexable phrase permutations, flagging waste (plural duplicates, words repeated across fields, auto-indexed free words like 'app'/'game') and simulating camelCase title splitting.
  WHY: Apple's documented match mechanics (token combination within a locale, plural stemming, cross-field dedup, capital-letter splitting) are deterministic rules that can be encoded once and run for free — but incumbents expose them only as human-readable 'best practice' checklists, not as machine-readable diagnostics. An agent calling Sniffy gets back 'these 7 characters are wasted, these 12 phrase combinations are newly reachable if you move word X to the subtitle' — computable, verifiable, and unique.
  DATA: Public metadata via iTunes lookup (free) + Apple's documented rules (already cited); no external API needed.
- (free-data) Review-velocity and rating-threshold signal: compute reviews-per-week trend per storefront and flag the <3.5 visibility cliff and >4.0 boost band, framed as 'review velocity often beats absolute rating' with provenance labels.
  WHY: Velocity (not just average rating) is the behavioral signal practitioners say matters, and it is fully derivable from public review feeds over time — but tools sell it inside seat-licensed dashboards (AppTweak, Appfigures) with no pay-per-request API. Sniffy already has rank history infrastructure; adding review-count snapshots to the same cache makes this nearly free and gives the paid report a credible behavioral section despite having no App Store Connect access.
  DATA: Public App Store review feeds/scrapes sampled over time (free); requires Sniffy's existing cache to accumulate history.
- (cheap-api) Tail-query semantic coverage score: embed the app's full public metadata and measure semantic fit against long-tail query clusters in its niche, explicitly framed around Apple's Feb 2026 finding that LLM relevance labels most improved tail-query ranking.
  WHY: Apple's own paper proves tail queries are now ranked by semantic textual relevance where behavioral incumbency is weak — that is precisely the terrain where an indie app can win against big incumbents. Sniffy already shipped embeddings in Phase 9, so this is a scoring/report change, not new infra; no competitor currently cites Apple's paper or operationalizes 'semantic coverage' as a number an agent can optimize against.
  DATA: Public metadata (free) + existing embeddings pipeline + a curated tail-query corpus per category (can be bootstrapped from autocomplete data Sniffy already collects).
- (hard) CPP-in-organic detection: for tracked keywords, detect when a competitor's search result shows a custom product page variant (screenshot set differs from their default product page), revealing exactly which keywords competitors are actively targeting with CPPs.
  WHY: CPPs ranking organically (July 2025) created a brand-new competitive intelligence surface: a CPP appearing for a keyword is a direct admission that the competitor assigned that keyword. AppTweak ships a 'CPP Explorer' but only inside its enterprise dashboard. Even a low-frequency sampled version would be a unique signal in Sniffy's competitor trail.
  DATA: Per-keyword App Store search result scraping plus screenshot-set diffing against the default product page; storefront-specific and rate-limit sensitive.
